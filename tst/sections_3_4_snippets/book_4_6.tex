Theorem 6. Let $J$ be a 1 -sphere in $\mathbf{R}^{2}$, and let $U$ be a component of $\mathbf{R}^{2}-J$. Then $J=\operatorname{Fr} U$.

Proof. Obviously $\operatorname{Fr} U \subset J$. If $\operatorname{Fr} U$ is not all of $J$, then $\operatorname{Fr} U$ lies in an arc $A$ in $J$. Since $\mathbf{R}^{2}-J$ has another component $V$, it follows that $A$ separates $\mathbf{R}^{2}$, which contradicts Theorem 5. \(\square\)
